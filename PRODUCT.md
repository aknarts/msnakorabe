# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Parents and grandparents of young children (ages 3-6) in Prague 8, Libeň and surrounding areas. They're researching kindergartens, comparing options, and making one of the first big decisions about their child's life outside the home. They arrive anxious, hopeful, and time-pressured. Many visit on mobile during commutes or late evenings.

## Product Purpose

Public-facing website for Mateřská škola Na Korábě. Serves as the school's digital front door: first impression, information hub, and trust-builder for prospective families. Success means a parent leaves feeling "this is the right place for my child" and takes the next step (visiting, enrolling, calling).

## Brand Personality

Warm, playful, trustworthy.

The voice is a kind teacher speaking to a new parent: reassuring but not stiff, enthusiastic but not breathless. The nautical metaphor (boat, voyage, crew) runs through everything as identity, not decoration.

Emotional goal: "This place feels special and full of life."

## Anti-references

- **Generic school templates**: Clip-art, dated WordPress themes, stock photos of children who clearly aren't from the school, Comic Sans or similar "kid" fonts. We are not a template.
- **Sterile institutional sites**: Government-style layouts, text walls, no personality, gray-on-white with a municipal crest. We are not a bureaucracy.
- **Over-designed startups**: Excessive animation, gradient-everything, "modern" tech aesthetics that signal software company, not kindergarten. We are not a SaaS product.

## Design Principles

1. **Nautical metaphor as identity**: The boat/ship/voyage theme isn't surface decoration. It shapes structure, copy, and visual rhythm. Every page is part of the journey.
2. **Warmth before formality**: Parents should feel welcomed, not processed. Every interaction prioritizes human connection over institutional authority.
3. **Show the life inside**: The site should feel alive, reflecting the energy and care that exists in the physical school. Static ≠ lifeless.
4. **Respect the reader**: Clear information architecture. No hunting for basics (hours, contacts, enrollment). Answer the anxious parent's questions before they need to ask.
5. **Craft over template**: Every element should feel intentional. If it looks assembled from a kit, it's wrong.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA compliance. The audience includes grandparents and non-native speakers; readability and clear hierarchy are non-negotiable. Reduced-motion preferences respected. Color contrast ratios maintained across all text. Czech language primary; no i18n required but clear copywriting assumed.
